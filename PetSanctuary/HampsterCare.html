Hampster Care

General Care Advice

Hamsters may be small, but they require thoughtful care to stay healthy and comfortable. Their diet should consist of a balanced hamster mix that includes grains, seeds, and small amounts of dried vegetables. Fresh vegetables can be offered occasionally, but avoid citrus, onions, and sugary foods. Hamsters have sensitive digestive systems, so it’s important not to change their food too quickly. Testing different mixes can help you find one your hamster enjoys, and brands like Oxbow and Kaytee are commonly recommended.

Hamsters need a spacious habitat with plenty of bedding for burrowing, which is a natural behavior that helps them feel safe. Deep bedding made from paper‑based materials is ideal, as wood shavings can sometimes cause respiratory issues. A solid exercise wheel is essential for their physical health, and chew toys help keep their teeth from overgrowing. Hamsters are nocturnal, so they are most active at night and should be handled gently during the day to avoid stress. Keeping their cage clean is important, but avoid removing all bedding at once, as this can cause anxiety—spot cleaning is usually enough.

Hamsters are solitary animals and should be housed alone to prevent fighting. They benefit from a quiet environment, as loud noises and sudden movements can stress them out. Regular but gentle handling helps them become more comfortable with you, but always let them wake up naturally before interacting. Watch for signs of illness such as wet tail, lethargy, or changes in appetite, and seek veterinary care if needed. With proper care, hamsters can live happy, active lives and make wonderful small pets.
