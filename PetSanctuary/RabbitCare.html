Rabbit Care

General Care Advice

Caring for rabbits requires more attention than many people realize, and small details in their daily routine can greatly improve their health and comfort. Rabbits rely heavily on a proper diet, and the majority of their food should be high‑quality hay, which keeps their digestive system functioning properly and helps wear down their constantly growing teeth. Fresh leafy greens should be offered daily, while pellets should be given in moderation. Avoid sugary treats and foods that can cause digestive issues. It’s important not to switch their diet too quickly, as rabbits have sensitive stomachs. Testing different types of hay, such as timothy, orchard grass, or meadow hay, can help you find what your rabbit prefers. Brands like Oxbow and Small Pet Select are widely recommended for both hay and pellets.

Rabbits need a clean, spacious environment to stay healthy. Their enclosure should be large enough for them to hop, stretch, and move freely, with solid flooring and soft bedding. Regular cleaning is essential, as dirty bedding can lead to respiratory issues and infections. Rabbits also need daily exercise outside their enclosure, ideally in a rabbit‑proofed area where they can explore safely. Providing tunnels, chew toys, and hiding spots helps keep them mentally stimulated and prevents boredom. Grooming is important as well, especially for long‑haired breeds, since rabbits groom themselves frequently and can develop dangerous hair blockages if not brushed regularly.

Rabbits are social animals that benefit from gentle handling and companionship, whether from humans or another rabbit. They communicate through subtle body language, so paying attention to their behavior can help you understand their needs. Regular vet checkups are important, especially since rabbits can hide signs of illness. Watch for changes in appetite, droppings, or activity levels, as these can indicate serious health issues like GI stasis. With proper care, rabbits can live long, healthy lives and form strong bonds with their caregivers.
