Guinea Pig Care

General Care Advice

Guinea pigs are social, vocal animals that thrive on companionship and consistent care. Their diet is extremely important, and they require unlimited access to high‑quality hay, which supports digestion and dental health. Guinea pigs cannot produce their own vitamin C, so they need daily servings of vitamin C‑rich vegetables like bell peppers, kale, and parsley. Pellets formulated specifically for guinea pigs can be offered in moderation, but avoid mixes with seeds or dried fruit. Brands like Oxbow and Sherwood are commonly recommended. It’s best not to change their food too quickly, as sudden changes can cause digestive issues.

Guinea pigs need a large, open‑top enclosure with soft bedding and plenty of space to move around. Avoid wire flooring, as it can injure their feet. They also need hideouts, tunnels, and chew toys to keep them mentally stimulated and comfortable. Regular cleaning is essential, as guinea pigs produce a lot of waste and can develop respiratory issues if their environment becomes dirty. They benefit from daily floor time outside their enclosure, where they can explore safely and interact with their caregivers.

Guinea pigs are gentle animals that communicate through a variety of sounds, from squeaks to purrs. They do best in pairs or small groups, as they are highly social and can become lonely when kept alone. Regular grooming, nail trimming, and monitoring their weight are important parts of their care. Watch for signs of illness such as changes in appetite, breathing difficulties, or unusual behavior. With proper care, guinea pigs can live long, healthy lives and form strong bonds with their owners.
